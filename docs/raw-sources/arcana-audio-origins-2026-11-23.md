# Arcana's `assets/audio/`: where each sound comes from and under what licence (#833)

- **Source:** the public repositories `ChipsAhoiMcCoy/arcana` (read at commit `eaa3ed41bad27fc585c28578bff9ac2e09fa591f`,
  `main`), `ahicks92/grimdark` (Grim Dark) and `bradjrenshaw/darkvision-wotr` (Dark Vision), read through the GitHub
  REST API with `gh api` (repository `contents`, `git/trees?recursive=1`, `commits?path=`, `commits/<sha>`). Read-only:
  no audio file was downloaded. Arcana's own notes are `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and `CREDITS.md`; the licence texts are
  `LICENSES/grim-dark-zlib.txt` and `LICENSES/dark-vision-zlib.txt`. The upstream trees were read at their `main` on
  the saved date.
- **Saved:** 2026-11-23.
- **Game build:** none (about other people's projects; nothing here is pinned to a Metaphor: ReFantazio build).

Arcana's code is Zlib (`gh api repos/ChipsAhoiMcCoy/arcana` reports `Zlib`); Grim Dark and Dark Vision are Zlib as well
(`Copyright (c) 2026 Austin Hicks` and `Copyright (c) 2026 Bradley Renshaw`, one repository-wide `LICENSE` each).
A Zlib repository licence covers a sound only if its owner made it or had the right to relicense it.

## Method

1. Listed every file under `assets/audio/` at the pinned commit (15 files; the git blob SHA is the SHA below).
2. Read Arcana's `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` (sections "Wall tones", "Sonar sounds from Grim Dark", "Code and sounds from
   Dark Vision") and `CREDITS.md`.
3. Matched each blob SHA against the Grim Dark and Dark Vision trees. Equal git blob SHAs mean byte-identical files.
   All 15 match at least one upstream file.
4. Read the upstream commit history of each match, and the upstream notes that name a sound's source.

Result of step 3: every Arcana sound is a byte-for-byte copy of a Grim Dark file, and 9 of the 15 are also in Dark
Vision, where they were committed first (Dark Vision 2026-06-08 to 2026-08-13, Grim Dark from 2026-08-22). So the
original author of those 9 is Dark Vision's owner, not Grim Dark's as Arcana's notice says for the sonar sounds.

## Evidence for the main groups

- **sight (3, reuse).** Arcana's notice says they are Grim Dark's harmful-ground sounds, made "with its own generator
  script". Grim Dark commit `1cbe1732e9` (author `ahicks92`) adds the three files and `tools/gen_hazard_cues.py` together,
  and the script's docstring describes them as synthesized (`sizzle`: "dense irregular impulses, ~35/s, 3 ms bursts";
  `inside_low`: "dense steady sizzle (80/s) pitched at 260 Hz"). Procedural output of a Zlib project's own script, under
  that project's licence. The script itself was not run to reproduce the bytes [NEEDS VERIFICATION that it does].
- **sonar `exit`, `object`, `event` (3, replace).** Arcana credits all five sonar sounds to Austin Hicks. His own
  documents name three of them after another pack: `se_old_pack00 door05` (exit), `se_old_pack00 buble05` (object),
  `push17` (event); the devlog says `buble05` was "copied into the repo". No pack, author or licence is named anywhere
  I read, and Dark Vision does not contain these three files. Treated as third-party until proven otherwise. Searching
  `pack00` in Dark Vision and Arcana found nothing.
- **sonar `fragment`, signals `shadow` (2, unclear).** Dark Vision's files, committed before Grim Dark had them (so
  Arcana's credit to Austin Hicks for these is wrong; the author is the Dark Vision owner or whoever he got them from).
  No origin stated. `4674ab13f5` shows they were 8-bit PCM until re-encoded, which is not how a modern generator
  writes. `fragment.wav` was also edited in `90fde46c22` "(user-authored)", an unnamed contributor.
- **walltones (4) and review pings (3), unclear.** The licence is plain: the owner's repository-wide Zlib, restated in
  Arcana's notice. What is missing is any statement of how they were made, so a sampled third-party origin is not ruled
  out. Grim Dark's `gen_hazard_cues.py` docstring says its loops are "built like the wall tones: one mono 4 s loop per
  compass direction, ... RMS -20 dBFS", which suggests the wall tones are synthesized, but it describes the hazards, not
  the wall tones, so it is not proof.
