# The Last of Us Part II: its accessibility options, from PlayStation's and Naughty Dog's own pages

- **Sources:** the five pages below, read with a fetch tool on 2026-11-21. Each section names its URL and the page's
  own date. Quotes are short; the option lists are paraphrased from the pages, in their order and with their names.
- **Saved:** 2026-11-21.
- **Game build:** none; this note is about another game (The Last of Us Part II, PS4, and its PS5 Remastered release),
  not Metaphor: ReFantazio.
- **Not reached / limits:** no GDC Vault session on Part II's accessibility turned up in two searches; developer talks
  were therefore not read, and nothing below rests on one. How any option was built is not on these pages and isn't
  claimed here. Press articles and fan wikis were not used. Nothing was downloaded or run; fetched text was treated as
  data.

## What the sources say overall

- Source 2: more than 60 settings; "60+ settings make this Naughty Dog's most accessible game yet." Three presets
  (vision, hearing, motor) set recommended options, and each option can still be changed after.
- Source 1, vision preset: "Configures all the recommended settings designed for players who are blind or have low
  vision."
- Source 2: text-to-speech narrates on-screen text and menus, and turns on by itself for English users with the
  console's text-to-speech on.
- Source 3: the consultants included Brandon Cole, credited with advice on distinct sound cues for telling attacks
  apart and locating opponents, and Steve Saylor, on zoom, narration, high contrast and text size. Developers quoted
  there speak about inclusive design in general, not how features were built.
- Source 4 (Part I): Game Director Matthew Gallant: "The biggest new feature we have are audio descriptions for
  cinematics." Setting text: "Enables descriptive audio during cinematics."
- Source 5 (Part II Remastered): "Descriptive Audio and Speech to Vibrations have also been added to Part II's suite of
  accessibility features." Descriptive Audio carries the footnote "Only available for audio supported languages".

## The option list (source 1, its categories; source 2 agrees where it covers the same option)

### Presets
- Vision, hearing and motor presets.

### Alternate controls
- Input remap: control scheme (default, right hand only, left hand only, three custom), remap every command (including
  touchpad swipes and controller shake), rename a scheme, controller orientation, swap sticks, boat controls,
  rope/ladder movement relative to character or camera, guitar strumming input, melee while aiming.
- Holds and presses: repeated button press (tap or hold), melee combos (tap or hold), and hold-or-toggle for aiming,
  listen mode, sprinting (patch 1.01), crafting, backpack weapon swap, hold breath; bow firing (hold to draw, or tap).
- Assistance: camera assist (turns the camera toward movement), auto weapon swap, auto pick-up (ammo and ingredients),
  lock-on aim (off, on, auto-target, which locks onto the next enemy even offscreen), lock-on strength 1-10, arc-throw
  lock-on and its strength.

### Magnification and visual aids
- HUD scale, HUD background, HUD color, HUD colorblind mode, HUD flashing.
- High contrast display: mutes the environment and emphasises characters and objects; touchpad swipe toggles it.
- Screen magnifier: off, low, medium, high, steered with the touchpad.
- Translation prompts for in-world text: off, on, auto.

### Motion sickness
- Camera shake, motion blur, dolly zoom, field of view, camera distance, full-screen effects, persistent centre dot.

### Navigation and traversal
- Navigation assistance: L3 turns the player toward the story path; with enhanced listen mode, toward the last scanned
  target.
- Traversal assistance: simplifies jumps, climbing, horse vaulting and sprinting in some encounters.
- Ledge guard: audio and vibration warnings, and it prevents falls from ledges that would be fatal.
- Infinite breath underwater.
- Skip puzzle option, from the pause menu; source 2: "highly recommended for players who are blind or have low
  vision."
- Enhanced listen mode: scan for items and enemies, with pitch giving a target's height relative to the player; scan
  range 10-30 m and scan time 1-5 s.

### Text-to-speech and audio cues
- Text-to-speech, with a touchpad swipe for a status description.
- Traversal cues: gaps, ledges, crouch and prone areas, pickups, breakable glass.
- Combat audio cues: enemy grabs, incoming melee, arrows, being aimed at, hits.
- Combat vibration cues: incoming melee, being aimed at, hits. Guitar vibration cues.
- Separate volumes for effects, dialogue, music, cinematics, accessibility cues and text-to-speech.
- Audio cue glossary: browse and listen to every cue.

### Combat accessibility (one switch turns these on)
- Hostages don't escape; allies don't get grabbed; enemies don't flank; reduced enemy perception; reduced enemy
  accuracy; enhanced dodge; invisible while prone (off, limited, unlimited, while prone and not aiming); weapon sway;
  slow motion (off, while aiming, toggle).

### HUD and subtitles
- Damage direction indicators; awareness indicators (off, stealth, always; patch 1.01); hints when lost (off,
  sometimes, frequent; L3); pick-up notifications; dodge prompts.
- Subtitles (off, story, story and combat dialogue), size, background, speaker names, name colours, direction arrow
  for offscreen speakers, colour. Source 2: subtitling systemic enemy dialogue in combat was a first for the studio.

### Difficulty
- Challenge presets (very light, light, moderate, hard, survivor, custom) and separate sliders for player, enemies,
  allies, stealth and resources.

### Later additions
- Grounded update (1.05): motion-sensor aiming, film grain.
- Remastered (source 5): descriptive audio for cinematics, speech to vibrations.
